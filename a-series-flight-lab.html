FLIGHT STAGE FACTS · EVERY STAGE OF THE SEQUENCE

A-SERIES FACT FILE · every vehicle in the series: initial thrust-to-weight ≈ 2:1 for A1, A3 and A4 · ≈ 2.9:1 for the lighter A2. The vehicle the current engine belongs to is highlighted.

A11.40 m · 150 kg · 3.0 kN → nominal T/W 2.0 (design figure — it never flew) · destroyed during ground testing in 1933; accounts differ on how it failed and how long it ran, so no flight data exist
A21.61 m · 107 kg · 3.0 kN → T/W 2.9, the highest of the series · two flights from Borkum, December 1934: apogees 2.2 km and 3.5 km, impacts ≈0.8 km and ≈0.5 km
A36.74 m · 750 kg · 14.7 kN → T/W 2.0 · four launch attempts, none controlled — the first ejected its recovery parachute early, the next three toppled into the sea · heights ≈0.4–0.9 km, no verified downrange distance
A414.03 m · 12,500 kg · 245 kN → T/W 2.0 · engine baseline: 65 s burn, ≈1,600 m/s at cutoff · operational profile: range ≈320 km · steep research flight: apogee ≈88 km · first successful test: ≈190 km range, ≈85 km apogee · the 1937 specification was drawn around ≈25 t at take-off; the vehicle as flown was ≈12.5 t

SOURCES · MODEL LIMITS · HISTORICAL RESPONSIBILITY

Historical responsibilityThe German Army developed the A4 as a long-range ballistic missile. Under the propaganda designation V-2 it was fired at populated areas, and serial production at Mittelwerk depended on forced labour under lethal conditions tied to the Mittelbau-Dora camp system — more people died building the weapon than were killed by it. The engineering history cannot responsibly be separated from the victims of production and deployment. Mittelbau-Dora Memorial · Imperial War Museums — V-weapons context.
